# swift-ballistics

Use this package when a thrown body should fly, slow and fall, and you need where it is at an instant without simulating the instants before it. Start from `Ballistics.Trajectory<Scalar>`, the motion along one axis made from the velocity it is thrown with, a constant acceleration and a linear drag, and ask for `position(at:)`, `velocity(at:)`, its `apex` and, with `landing(at:)`, the time it reaches a position on the way down. Each answer is a formula in the time, so frames can be computed alone, out of order and in parallel, and always agree. The scalar supplies `exp`, `expm1` and `log1p` through `Exponential.Protocol`; this package conforms no type. For a body on a spring, use swift-spring.

## Laws

- The motion is the exact solution of v′ = a − k × v along one axis: a constant acceleration a and a drag k in proportion to the velocity, Stokes's linear drag. A trajectory has a finite velocity, a finite acceleration and a finite drag of zero or more; construction fails with `.velocity`, `.acceleration` or `.drag`, checked in that order. With drag the velocity tends to the terminal velocity a / k; without drag it is the motion under constant acceleration, and the answers for a small drag approach those without.
- The axes do not interact under this drag. A flight in the plane or in space is one trajectory per axis with the same drag: the acceleration along the pull, zero across it. Along an axis without acceleration the position tends to v / k.
- `position(at:)` is v × t × φ₁(k × t) + a × t² × φ₂(k × t), measured from where it was thrown, with φ₁(z) = (1 − e^(−z)) / z and φ₂(z) = (z − 1 + e^(−z)) / z², which are 1 and 1/2 at zero; φ₂ is summed as its Taylor series for |z| < 1/8, where the direct form cancels. `velocity(at:)` is v × e^(−k × t) + a × t × φ₁(k × t), its derivative. At time zero they are zero and the velocity it was thrown with.
- `apex` is the time at which the velocity is zero, log(1 − k × v / a) / k, or −v / a without drag, when it is thrown against the acceleration, and zero otherwise.
- `landing(at:)` is the one time after the apex at which the position is the given one, moving with the acceleration. It is `nil` for a position behind the position at the apex, which it never reaches after it, and without acceleration; the position at the apex gives the apex. Without drag it is the apex plus √(2 × (position − position at apex) / a). With drag it is found by halving a bracket sure to hold it, the apex and the apex plus (position − position at apex) × k / a + 1 / k, until no scalar lies between the two ends, and is the later end.
- Results are as repeatable across platforms as the scalar's `exp`, `expm1` and `log1p`.
